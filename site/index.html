How it works

  1. 1

    Pick what to move

    Open Offload with the account that’s full. Choose whole folders, your largest files, or photos and videos. Sizes are shown so you know what you’ll free.

  2. 2

    Send it to your other account

    Enter the Gmail address you’re moving to and send. Offload asks Google to transfer ownership of every file, subfolders included.

  3. 3

    Accept from the other account

    Open Offload signed in as the receiving account and accept everything in one go. The files now count against that account’s storage, not the first one.

Questions

Is Offload free?

Yes. It's free to use, with no sign-up beyond the Google account you already have.

Where do my files go? Does anyone else see them?

Nowhere new. Offload runs as a Google Apps Script inside your own Google account and uses Google Drive's built-in ownership transfer. Files never pass through any other server, and the developer can't see them.

Why does Google say the app isn't verified?

Google shows this for apps that haven't been through its review yet. To continue, choose Advanced, then Go to Offload (unsafe). The app needs access to your Drive so it can list your files and hand ownership over.

Does the other account have to accept?

Yes. Google requires the receiving account to accept each transfer. Offload's Waiting for you tab lets them accept everything at once, and loose files are tidied into a folder named after the sender.

Can it move photos from the Google Photos app?

No. Google doesn't allow transferring items that live only in Google Photos. Offload moves photos and videos stored in Google Drive.

Does it work with work or school accounts?

Usually not. Most organisations block ownership transfer outside their domain. Offload is built for personal Gmail accounts.

I accepted the files. Why hasn't my storage changed?

Google can take a few hours to update storage figures. Also empty the trash in the account the files came from.
